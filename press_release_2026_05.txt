NEARLY 1 IN 3 TEXAS LICENSED CONTRACTORS OPERATING WITH EXPIRED BONDS,
NEW REAL-TIME COMPLIANCE TRACKER REVEALS

First-ever statewide dashboard exposing bond expiration gaps among 775,000+
TDLR-licensed Texas contractors and 558,000+ commissioned notaries

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

DALLAS, Texas — May 26, 2026 — Quantum Surety LLC, a Texas-licensed surety bond
agency, today launched Texas Bond Watch (quantumsurety.bond/texas-bond-watch), the
state's first real-time public tracker of contractor and notary bond expirations —
and the data reveals a significant compliance gap that puts Texas consumers at risk.

KEY FINDINGS FROM THE TEXAS BOND WATCH DATABASE:

• 226,767 Texas licensed contractors (29.3%) are currently operating with expired
  surety bonds — meaning they are out of compliance with TDLR licensing requirements
• An additional 34,480 Texas contractors have bonds expiring in the next 30 days
• 95,375 Texas notaries (17.1%) have expired bonds
• Harris County alone has 6,282 contractors with bonds expiring in the next 30 days
• Dallas County: 3,583 contractors expiring within 30 days
• Tarrant County (Fort Worth): 2,329 contractors expiring within 30 days

"Most Texas homeowners assume that a licensed contractor is automatically bonded
and insured," said a spokesperson for Quantum Surety LLC. "What they don't realize
is that a license doesn't expire when a bond does. Contractors can continue operating
— and advertising as 'licensed' — even after their bond lapses. That leaves consumers
with no financial recourse if something goes wrong on their project."

ABOUT TEXAS BOND WATCH

Texas Bond Watch (quantumsurety.bond/texas-bond-watch) is a free public tool that
pulls live data daily from two Texas state databases:

  — Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR): 775,171 licensed
    contractor records covering electricians, HVAC technicians, plumbers, roofers,
    and 200+ other trade license types
  — Texas Secretary of State (SOS): 558,898 commissioned notary records

The platform provides:
  • County-by-county drilldown for the 10 largest Texas counties
  • Real contractor and notary names, license numbers, and exact expiration dates
  • A free embeddable widget for title companies, real estate offices, and property
    managers to verify contractor compliance on their own websites
  • Email alert subscriptions for consumers who want to be notified when a specific
    contractor's bond is about to expire
  • A free bond status badge that contractors can display on their own websites

WHY BOND STATUS MATTERS

In Texas, most contractor licenses require an active surety bond as a condition
of licensure. A surety bond is a financial guarantee that protects consumers if
a contractor fails to complete a job, causes property damage, or violates licensing
laws. Without an active bond, a consumer who hires an unlicensed or lapsed-bond
contractor may have no legal recourse beyond civil court.

"We built this because the data exists publicly — it's just not usable," the
spokesperson continued. "TDLR and the Texas SOS both publish their license
databases, but there's no tool that puts it all together and shows you which
contractors are compliant right now, by ZIP code or county. We built that tool,
and we're giving it away free."

THE CONTRACTOR BADGE SYSTEM

As part of the launch, Quantum Surety is also introducing the first free bond
verification badge system for Texas contractors (quantumsurety.bond/badge). Similar
to SSL certificate trust seals used on websites, licensed contractors and notaries
can embed a live badge showing their current bond status — with data that updates
daily from state records.

"A contractor who is actively bonded should be proud to show it," the spokesperson
said. "This badge gives them a way to do that — and it gives consumers an instant
way to verify compliance before signing any contract."
