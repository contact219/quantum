MORE THAN 1,700 TEXAS AUTO DEALERS OPERATING WITH EXPIRED SURETY BONDS,
DATA ANALYSIS REVEALS — CAR BUYERS MAY HAVE REDUCED LEGAL PROTECTION
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Free Online Database Lets Texans Verify Any Dealer's Bond Status Before Signing;
GDN Compliance Rates Among Worst in Texas

DALLAS, TX, June 24, 2026 — A new analysis of Texas Department of Motor
Vehicles (TxDMV) licensing records shows that at least 1,777 Texas auto
dealers are currently operating with expired GDN (General Distinguishing
Number) surety bonds — bonds legally required to protect consumers in
vehicle sale transactions.

The findings come from Quantum Surety LLC, a Texas-licensed surety bond
agency (TDI License #3480229) that operates verify.quantumsurety.bond, a
free public database covering 19,700+ Texas auto dealer records and
816,000+ TDLR contractor license records updated daily from public sources.

KEY FINDINGS (from TxDMV public data as of June 2026):
  - 1,777 Texas auto dealers: surety bonds expired
  - 2,283 Texas auto dealers: bonds expiring within 90 days
  - ~18.9% of all Texas GDN licensees: in or near non-compliance
  - Texas law requires all GDN dealers to maintain a $50,000 surety bond

"When you buy a car from a Texas dealer, their surety bond is your
safety net," said Theodore Sparks, founder of Quantum Surety LLC. "If a
dealer commits fraud, fails to deliver title, or takes your money and
folds, you can file a claim against that bond to recover your loss. If
the bond has lapsed, that protection doesn't exist — even if the dealer
is still licensed on paper."

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR TEXAS CAR BUYERS

Under Texas Transportation Code § 503.033, all Texas GDN license holders
are required to maintain an active surety bond as a condition of their
license. The required bond amount is $50,000, backed by an approved
insurance carrier.

A lapsed bond does not automatically trigger license suspension — which
means dealers can and do continue operating after their bond expires.
Consumers have no way to check this without tools like the one Quantum
Surety provides free of charge.
